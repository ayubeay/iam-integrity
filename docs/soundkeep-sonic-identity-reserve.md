# RESERVE - SoundKeep Sonic Identity (Echo Rings)

**Status:** Reserved visual-language direction.
**Urgency:** LOW as animation. MEDIUM as a design decision, because it constrains what gets
built elsewhere.

## The idea
Concentric rings radiating from the source, behaving like sound propagating rather than
decorating a page. Each beat births a ring at the centre; it expands, cools through the
palette, and dissolves into particles at the edge.

    centre   white-blue
             electric blue
             azure
             indigo
    edge     purple-blue, dissolving to transparent

## Not a loop
Real music is not perfectly repetitive, and identical pulses read as a GIF. Rings should
respond to what is playing:

    calm            slow, wide, thin pulses
    high energy     faster, tighter
    bass drop       one bright ring travelling further before fading
    build-up        pulses closer together, accelerating
    drop            rings widen dramatically, then settle

Palette may shift by genre - afrobeats gold and warm orange, tech house blue through
purple, dancehall green and red accents, ambient indigo and violet.

## Why it is more than decoration
The rings can become SoundKeep's visual language, used consistently rather than as one
flashy hero:

    home              introduce featured collections
    track preview     pulse with the audio
    recommendations   reorganise to show musical similarity
    Practice Decks    visualise the OVERLAP between outgoing and incoming tracks

That last one is the strongest. A DJ seeing two ring systems interfere - constructive where
they blend, chaotic where they clash - would get an intuitive read on a transition that no
number conveys. It turns a brand motif into an instrument.

## Grading
The hero animation is polish and can wait. But deciding now that echo rings ARE the visual
language affects what gets built: the transition-overlap visualisation in Practice Decks is
a real feature, and it only makes sense if the motif is established.

Worth committing to the language before building the animation.
