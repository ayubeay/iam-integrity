# RESERVE - Creative Identity Graph

**Status:** Reserved concept beneath SoundKeep and Sound-as-a-Service.
**Urgency:** LOW to build. MEDIUM as a watch item - see the MySpace note.

## The idea
A musician should not merely have an account. Their identity should be a graph of actual
creative relationships:

    artist <-> songs <-> collaborators <-> projects <-> credits <-> rights
           <-> versions <-> performances <-> fans and communities

And those relationships carry receipts.

    your creative identity is not your follower count.
    it is the body of work and the relationships you can prove.

## Why now
MySpace's owners have publicly stated an intent to relaunch, arguing that contemporary social
media became too dominated by algorithms and engagement optimisation. No date announced.

MySpace's strongest historical property was not social networking. It was **identity through
creativity** - an artist's page was not interchangeable, music lived on the identity, fans
discovered artists socially, and musicians built audiences without first becoming
conventional content creators.

That validates part of the SoundKeep thesis from outside.

## The layer distinction
    MySpace    artist <-> fan <-> social identity <-> discovery
    SoundKeep  music <-> creation <-> preservation and workflow <-> artist
    SaaS2      artist <-> producer <-> songwriter <-> vocalist <-> engineer
               <-> rights <-> licensing <-> royalties <-> delivery

    VERITY + IAM + receipts:
      who created it? who owns what? who changed what? who approved what?
      which version became the master? what rights were granted? what payment is owed?

That is a different infrastructure position, one layer deeper than a social feed.

## Posture
Watch the relaunch closely. Treat it as external validation for creator-centred identity and
as a potential future DISTRIBUTION surface rather than a competitor - if they rebuild the
artist and fan layer, SoundKeep does not need to own it. SoundKeep can be the professional
infrastructure underneath creators who publish outward.

**Do not build a MySpace clone. Do not divert engineering.**

Worth flagging if their relaunch exposes APIs, artist tools, creator monetisation, rights
infrastructure or developer integrations.
