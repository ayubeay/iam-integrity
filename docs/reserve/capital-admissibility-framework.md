# RESERVE — Capital Admissibility Framework

Status: Reserve Only
Captured: 2026-07-17

## Vision

Traditional fundraising focuses on convincing investors. Future capital
systems determine whether a project is ADMISSIBLE for capital allocation
through observable execution rather than persuasion. Capital becomes
another execution resource governed by evidence — allocated like compute,
bandwidth, storage, permissions, and execution time: through governance,
not enthusiasm.

Investment should not begin with "who tells the best story?" but with "who
has demonstrated the highest probability of successful execution?"
**Execution precedes valuation.**

## Admissibility dimensions

**Mission** (objective pursued). **Market** (problem, who experiences it,
demand evidence). **Execution** (what already exists: working software,
receipts, benchmarks, deployments, users, revenue, operational history).
**Allocation** (exactly where funds go: engineering, infrastructure,
sales, compliance, operations, acquisition). **Milestones** (capital
supports measurable execution events — MVP complete, first paying
customer, production deployment, first enterprise integration — not
arbitrary time periods).

## Staged deployment

Instead of raise -> receive -> spend:
Milestone -> Verification -> Capital Release -> Execution -> Receipt ->
Next Milestone. **Capital follows execution rather than speculation.**

## Integration

VERITY evaluates execution quality, not pitch quality (receipts,
deployment history, adoption, operational metrics, technical verification,
governance compliance). OROS coordinates staged releases. IAM identifies
requesting and approving parties. LITMUS enforces constitutional funding
policies. HelixAtlas visualizes funding as execution flow: source,
milestone achieved, capital deployed, resulting execution, measurable
outcomes.

## Future capital classes

Seed (initial validation); Milestone (released only after verified goals);
Infrastructure (compute, networking, cloud, hardware, scaling); Community
(transparent governance involving users/contributors); Autonomous (agents
may RECOMMEND but never independently authorize — final authorization
remains governed by policy and evidence).

## Capital receipts

Every allocation documents: source, governing policy, admissibility
decision, milestone funded, approved budget, execution obligations,
outcome. Capital becomes auditable the same way execution is auditable.

## Long-term vision

Not "who deserves investment?" but **"which execution has earned additional
resources?"** (Origin note: the useful part of fundraising threads is never
the money source — it's that the real questions are execution questions:
what are you building, who needs it, how will you acquire customers, where
does the money go.)

## Extension 2026-08-29 — Outcome Optionality & Capital-Induced Outcome Compression

Status: RESERVED — DO NOT BUILD. Architectural refinement of this reserve.

### Why this belongs here and not in its own file

This document already asks whether a project is admissible for capital, and answers it
across Mission, Market, Execution, Allocation and Milestones. What it did not carry is
the mirror question: **not what the capital enables, but what accepting it forecloses.**
That is the same project-level admissibility decision seen from the other side, so it is
recorded here rather than as a fourth capital parent. The capital separation is
unchanged: this reserve owns project admissibility, `domain-aware-capital-intelligence.md`
owns evaluator-level intelligence, `governed-capital-eligibility.md` owns execution
financing, `transaction-gap-financing-primitive.md` owns the gap-shaped decision, and
`future-rights-exchange.md` owns future contractual rights — which ordinary fundraising
mechanics must not contaminate.

### Outcome Optionality — a sixth admissibility dimension

**Outcome Optionality.** Which economically meaningful futures remain reachable, and
remain worth reaching, after the proposed financing.

    What future outcomes become unavailable, unattractive, or economically
    insufficient if this capital is accepted today?

### Capital-induced outcome compression

A financing event is not only a cash movement. It changes the reachable outcome set:

    capital accepted
    → ownership changes
    → seniority and preferences created
    → governance and control changes
    → burn and growth commitments
    → follow-on financing expectations
    → stakeholder return requirements
    → minimum attractive exit shifts
    → previously acceptable outcomes become insufficient

**Capital can purchase execution capacity while simultaneously reducing strategic outcome
optionality.** The defect named here is **unpriced optionality loss** — not the presence
of outside capital. Accepting substantial capital is entirely rational where market
capture, infrastructure, regulation, network effects, R&D or speed genuinely require it.
This is neither anti-investor nor pro-bootstrapping doctrine.

### Dimensions — deliberately not a scalar

Ownership and dilution · liquidation seniority and preferences · participation and other
economic rights · governance and control consequences · capital actually required to
unlock the target milestone · implied future financing dependency · burn commitments ·
plausible exit-value distribution · founder and common waterfall · employee-equity
waterfall · investor return requirements · acquisition ranges that remain economically
meaningful · ability to remain independent · ability to raise less · ability to delay ·
alternative mechanisms · strategic options gained · strategic options eliminated.

**Do not compress these into a score.** The same restraint applies here as in
`independent-validation-capability-promotion.md` — *do not prematurely lock to one
number* — and for the same reason: a premature scalar destroys the structure the
assessment exists to expose.

### Distinctions that must not collapse

    capital raised      ≠ value captured
    valuation           ≠ liquidity
    revenue             ≠ distributable stakeholder value
    acquisition         ≠ successful outcome for every security class
    company success     ≠ identical stakeholder outcome
    fundraising event   ≠ execution milestone accomplished

A company may be operationally successful while a given security class receives little,
because the waterfall rather than the outcome determines distribution.

### Counterfactual capital decision

A financing decision is evaluated against alternatives, not in isolation:

    raise the proposed amount · raise less · raise later · a different instrument
    · finance the specific bottleneck · fund through customers or revenue · do not raise

Compared on the downstream **execution and ownership states** each path produces, not on
runway alone. Where the true constraint is a small verified temporary bottleneck, route
it to `transaction-gap-financing-primitive.md` rather than to permanent dilution.

### The financing thesis is a prospective claim

    "We are accepting this capital because it is expected to unlock X under conditions Y."

    financing thesis → capital deployment → milestone evidence
    → actual economic consequences → counterfactual comparison

This is an instance of the primitive `prospective-claim-commitment.md` already owns; it
is not a new commitment mechanism and is not restated here. Its integrity is governed by
`evidence-commitment-and-anchoring.md`, whose separation settles the central rule:

    a funding announcement is EVIDENCE that capital was raised
    it is not INTERPRETATION that the milestone was unlocked
    it is not EXECUTION OUTCOME

**The funding announcement must never satisfy the claim.** Today's financing receipt is
tomorrow's evidence, and is evaluated as such.

### Capital receipt — extended fields

The Capital receipts section above already records source, governing policy, admissibility
decision, milestone funded, approved budget, execution obligations and outcome. Where
outcome optionality is assessed, extend that record with: capital condition · instrument ·
necessity evidence · ownership transferred · seniority and preferences created ·
governance changes · follow-on financing implied · plausible outcome range before and
after financing · stakeholder waterfall scenarios · strategic options gained and lost ·
counterfactual alternatives considered · outcome optionality retained.

### Attention boundary

Large raises, high valuations, revenue headlines and acquisition announcements are
high-salience signals that do not independently establish stakeholder economic success.

    HEADLINE MAGNITUDE  ≠  ECONOMIC OUTCOME QUALITY

This is the capital instance of the invariant in
`decision-leverage-attention-admissibility.md`, and an adversarial case for it.

### Evidence boundary

The originating discussion reported a company raising a substantial sum, reaching material
revenue, and being acquired, with one early investor stating they received one times their
investment after six years. **That material establishes no acquisition price, capitalization
table, liquidation preference, debt, participation right, founder proceeds, employee
proceeds or secondary transaction.** The mechanism stands on its own logic; the case is an
**unverified illustration** and no claim about any party's proceeds is recorded as fact.
