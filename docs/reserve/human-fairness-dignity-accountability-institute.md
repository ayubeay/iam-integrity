# RESERVE — Human Fairness, Dignity & Accountability Institute (HFAI)

Working acronym: HFAI. Alternative working name: Human Fairness & Accountability
Observatory.
Status: RESERVED — research / institutional design only.
Classification: independent ethical institution / physical-world accountability
architecture.
Captured: 2026-08-28.

**DO NOT activate, incorporate, fundraise, hire, investigate people, or make public
accusations from this reserve.**

## Origin

How should fairness, accountability and evidence governance operate when consequential
actions leave software and affect people in the physical world?

The existing architecture already explores `observation → evidence → admissibility →
authorization → execution → receipt`. The physical world introduces the harder problem:
what happens when power affects a human being, a community, an animal, the environment,
property, livelihood, bodily safety, or access to essential resources?

Eventual examples may include worker mistreatment, unpaid labour, wrongful platform or
institutional action, housing displacement, physical injury, environmental contamination,
autonomous-machine incidents, abuse of institutional power, exploitative contracts,
coercion, unsafe working conditions, discriminatory treatment, denial of legitimate
remedy, destruction of community resources, and failures by powerful organizations to
correct established harm.

**The institution does not begin by assuming who is guilty. Its first responsibility is
to establish what actually happened.**

## Core mission

A future independent ethical institution whose purpose is to help protect human dignity,
legitimate rights, bodily safety, meaningful agency, truthful treatment, procedural
fairness, access to remedy, and the underlying conditions necessary for a wholesome human
life.

It must be capable of defending individuals and communities where consequential power is
exercised over them, while remaining equally committed to preventing false accusations,
manipulated evidence and unjustified conclusions.

Core doctrine: **DEFEND THE HUMAN, NOT THE NARRATIVE.**

No institution, government, corporation, political ideology, social movement, donor,
investigator, complainant or accused party receives automatic moral correctness.
**Evidence governs findings.**

## The Wholesome Human Condition

Do not define "wholesome" as one prescribed way of living. Different cultures,
individuals and communities may legitimately pursue different conceptions of the good
life.

Instead reserve a framework describing conditions whose *unjustified destruction*
materially reduces human agency or dignity: bodily safety · food · potable water ·
shelter · health-supporting environment · dignity · autonomy · privacy · freedom from
coercion · livelihood · meaningful opportunity · family and community connection · access
to knowledge · education · freedom of legitimate movement · environmental integrity ·
ability to participate in society · ability to contest consequential decisions · ability
to seek remedy after harm.

**The institution must not assign people a universal "wholesomeness score."** The
framework describes conditions, not individuals.

## Evidence pipeline

    witness / observation → evidence → provenance → context
    → applicable rights, duties and rules → causal responsibility
    → counterevidence / contestation → finding → receipt → follow-up
    → systemic learning

Evidence states must preserve uncertainty rather than resolve it prematurely:

    OBSERVED · AUTHENTICATED · CORROBORATED · CONTESTED · INCOMPLETE
    · ALTERED · UNVERIFIABLE · MISATTRIBUTED

Finding states:

    SUPPORTED · CONTESTED · UNRESOLVED · UNKNOWN · DISCONFIRMED

Responsibility roles are distinct and must not be collapsed into a single verdict:

    CONTRIBUTED · AUTHORIZED · ORDERED · EXECUTED · SUPERVISED · BENEFITED · REMEDIATED

Findings are ethical or contractual determinations. **They are not `GUILTY` / `NOT
GUILTY`** — the institution does not claim judicial authority.

## Machine involvement

Where an AI or autonomous machine is involved, **"the machine acted" never terminates the
accountability graph.** The graph continues backward through whoever designed, deployed,
configured, authorized, supervised or knowingly maintained the relevant execution
authority, while preserving uncertainty wherever responsibility genuinely cannot be
established.

    human / organizational intent → authority → model / software decision
    → data / sensor input → machine command → actuation → physical event
    → consequence → response

This is the institutional counterpart to the machine-sovereignty doctrine in
`docs/reserve/human-machine-sovereignty-boundary.md`: **HMSB owns the underlying
non-delegable-authority doctrine; HFAI owns the institutional remedy and appeal
function.** They must not be merged.

## Access to remedy

Remedy reachability is itself measurable: decision visibility · explanation access ·
evidence access · response capacity · independent review · remedy reachability · cost
proportionality · retaliation protection · accessibility.

A right that cannot be exercised in practice is not a protection.

## Protecting people from false accusation

Protecting humanity includes protecting humans from unjust accusation. Where evidence
establishes fabricated claims, edited media, mistaken identity, coordinated
misinformation, malicious complaint campaigns, falsely attributed machine actions or
incorrect institutional records, **the organization must document that finding.**

**Human rights are not protected by sacrificing truth for advocacy.**

## Whistleblower and witness protection

Future design should investigate confidential reporting, source protection, evidence
escrow, legal referral, retaliation monitoring, controlled identity disclosure,
cryptographic evidence commitments, independent timestamping and secure communications.

**No promise of anonymity should be made unless the infrastructure can actually support
it.**

## The institution must itself be accountable

This is load-bearing. The organization could accumulate enormous informational and moral
power. Therefore: **THE DEFENDER MUST BE DEFENSIBLE AGAINST.**

Governance requirements to preserve: transparent methodology · published
conflict-of-interest policy · financial transparency · donor independence · separation
between evidence collection and final adjudication where appropriate · right of response ·
documented dissent · appeals · corrections and correction history · investigator
accountability · privacy controls · independent external audits · internal whistleblower
protection · rotating governance · bounded executive authority · published evidentiary
thresholds · no undisclosed donor control over findings.

**No permanent moral authority.** The institution must never claim universal infallible
ethical authority. Its authority comes from evidence quality, transparent method,
procedural fairness and willingness to correct itself — not from prestige, funding,
political alignment, founder status or moral branding.

## Political neutrality

Human-rights work intersects with political controversy. The institution must not become
an instrument of a party, ideology, government or faction.

Neutrality does **not** mean pretending all claims are equally supported. It means using
the same evidence standards regardless of who benefits from the conclusion. When evidence
strongly supports one side, say so. When evidence is insufficient, preserve uncertainty.

## Adversarial tests before activation

Test the methodology against synthetic cases where: the apparent victim fabricated
evidence · the apparently powerful party was correct · both sides contributed to harm ·
authentic media was contextually misleading · important evidence arrived late · an
investigator had a conflict of interest · donor pressure favoured one outcome · public
opinion strongly favoured the wrong conclusion · an AI-generated evidence summary
contained a critical error · the accused supplied decisive counterevidence · the
responsible actor was a third party · causality remained unknowable.

**A trustworthy institution must survive cases where the morally satisfying answer is not
the evidentially supported answer.**

## First-phase activation standard

Do not begin with global claims about defending all humanity. If activated, begin
narrowly. A possible first research vertical is worker payment and procedural fairness,
because the evidence is comparatively structured:

    contract → work performed → time / outputs → payment obligation
    → payment or nonpayment → dispute → remedy

That could produce methodological evidence before entering harder domains involving
bodily injury, policing, healthcare, international conflict or political disputes. **This
is not a commitment that worker disputes will be the first vertical.**

## Canonical principles

Truth before accusation. Dignity before convenience. Fair procedure before institutional
power. Evidence before narrative. Unknown before manufactured certainty. Remedy after
established harm. Accountability for the powerful and the defender alike. Technology
should increase human agency, not quietly erase it.

Operating doctrine:

> Truth before action. Dignity during action. Accountability after action. Remedy after
> harm. Learning before repetition.

And the constitutional boundary:

> The organization should become powerful enough to help a person challenge power, but
> never powerful enough that people cannot challenge the organization itself.

## Activation rule

RESERVE ONLY. Do not establish the organization · solicit donations · investigate named
individuals · issue public accusations · claim legal authority · claim human-rights
certification authority · create surveillance infrastructure · build predictive policing ·
score human moral worth · build social-credit systems · create political blacklists · or
turn vLOID into a morality engine.

Activate only after a dedicated comparative research phase establishes: a repeated human
harm or fairness gap · inadequacy or inaccessibility of existing remedies · a defensible
methodological advantage · genuine procedural neutrality · sustainable independent
governance · privacy and evidence-protection capability · independent appeal and
correction mechanisms · a bounded first problem that can be handled competently.

At activation, begin with research and controlled case simulations before accepting
consequential real-world disputes.

## One-line definition

Human Fairness, Dignity & Accountability Institute: a future independent,
evidence-governed institution for protecting human dignity and meaningful agency by
reconstructing consequential physical-world events, preserving procedural fairness for
every party, tracing responsibility through human and machine systems, enabling realistic
remedy, and remaining itself transparent, challengeable and accountable.

RESERVED — DO NOT BUILD.
