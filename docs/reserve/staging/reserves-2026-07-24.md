## 1. Innovation alone is not the moat

Building something novel is not a defensible position by itself; novelty is
copied. The moat is the compounding asset around the innovation —
operational knowledge, execution receipts, verified reliability history,
distribution, trust. Recurring across the ecosystem: API Connect's value is
not "another data proxy" but the accumulated routing/reliability
intelligence; HELIX's value is not "an agent marketplace" but verified
execution history. Doctrine: when evaluating any build, ask what compounds
and is hard to copy, not merely what is new.

## 2. Evaluator–domain compatibility

An evaluator/scoring system is only meaningful within the domain its
evidence actually covers. A metric valid in one regime silently misleads in
another (lived precedent: the OHLCV shadow gates were valid for the pool-
indexed population but the candidate population sat outside that domain;
the criterion worked precisely because it did not pretend otherwise). Before
trusting any evaluator — trading, capability, provider quality, agent
scoring — confirm its evidence domain matches the population being judged.
Connects to the Regime Evidence Engine and Proof Before Promotion reserves.

## 3. Economic buyer vs operational pain owner

The party who FEELS a pain is often not the party who PAYS to fix it. A
durable product identifies both and the path between them; solving an
operational pain whose owner cannot authorize spend produces usage without
revenue. Relevant to any future commercialization of the execution stack
(API Connect, HELIX, Commerce Sniper): map the operational pain owner to
the economic buyer explicitly before building a paid offering.

## 4. Distribution as part of system endurance

Distribution is not a post-build marketing step bolted on at the end — it
is part of whether a system survives. A correct system nobody can find or
adopt does not endure. Belongs with the SURVIVOR endurance doctrine:
survivability spans execution reliability AND reachability/adoption. Design
for how the system is discovered and adopted as a first-class concern, not
an afterthought.

## 5. Raw-data → observation → inference → decision lineage

Every decision should be traceable back through a clean lineage: raw data →
observation → inference → decision → outcome. Each stage is a distinct
artifact; collapsing them hides where a decision actually came from. This is
the general form behind the receipt/telemetry work across the stack
(execution receipts, capability-routing receipts, the Information
Admissibility Governor's evidence pipeline). Doctrine: preserve the lineage
as separable artifacts so any decision can be audited and replayed from its
inputs. Candidate seed for the "Inference Evidence Ledger" canonical reserve
when its packet arrives.
