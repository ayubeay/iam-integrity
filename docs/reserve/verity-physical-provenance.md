# RESERVE - VERITY Physical Provenance & Producer Discovery

**Status:** Reserved. Dormant until an active product needs verified physical sourcing.

## The observed loop
    producer exists -> buyer cannot reliably discover them -> intermediaries fill the gap
    -> producer identity becomes obscured -> authenticity harder to establish
    -> buyer carries sourcing risk -> genuine producer captures less value

Observed via a buyer seeking authentic handloom products needing actual weavers plus
quality, authenticity and certification evidence. **The insight is not textiles.**

## Evidence questions, not verdicts
Who claims to have produced it? Is that identity established? What supports origin and
production method? What material and certification claims exist, and who issued them? How
current is the evidence? What transformations occurred after production? Who held custody at
each stage? Where are the gaps, contradictions and unverifiable claims?

## Evidence objects
PRODUCER_IDENTITY, WORKSHOP_IDENTITY, CAPABILITY_EVIDENCE, MATERIAL_EVIDENCE,
ORIGIN_EVIDENCE, PROCESS_EVIDENCE, CERTIFICATION, SAMPLE, INSPECTION, PRODUCTION_BATCH,
CUSTODY_EVENT, LOGISTICS_EVENT, BUYER_ACCEPTANCE.

Each retains issuer, timestamp, subject, evidence reference, confidence and verification
status. **A certification is evidence, not truth by declaration.** Missing evidence stays
distinguishable from contradictory or failed evidence.

## Economic doctrine
Do not design extraction into the architecture. A successful system improves both sides -
the buyer gets discovery, authenticity confidence and execution reliability; the producer
gets persistent verified identity, reputation, provenance history and buyer access.

## Explicit non-goals
Not a textile marketplace, not a sari startup, not an Earthwise project, not another
standalone application, not blockchain-for-every-product, and not a requirement that every
physical object carry exhaustive provenance.

## Principle
Physical provenance is an evidence problem before it is a marketplace problem. Discovery
finds a producer; provenance establishes why that producer and product should be trusted;
governance determines what may happen because of that evidence.

When activated, begin with the smallest evidence graph that resolves a real transaction.
