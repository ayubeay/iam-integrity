## Method - dependency intelligence
Traditional search asks *what exists?* Zircon asks *what depends on this?* and then *what
depends on those dependencies?* - building a living graph across technologies, materials,
regulation, manufacturing, economics, logistics, workforce, research, software and
infrastructure.

The output is an engineering pathway, not a literature summary.

    NASA thermal material -> which industries benefit, which products improve, what
    certifications are required, what manufacturing changes, what supply chain
    dependencies, who should collaborate

    corrosion-resistant alloy from oil and gas -> offshore wind? EV battery manufacturing?
    Earthwise? HELIOS Airborne? which companies should investigate?

Also human capital as knowledge infrastructure: what expertise is missing, which scientists
to recruit, which universities produce useful research, which disciplines are converging.
R&D workforce intelligence, not HR.

## Subordinate research programs
**(a) Cross-Domain Dependency Intelligence** - second-order consequence mapping across
technology, infrastructure, finance, engineering, science and government. Feeds VERITY,
HelixShield, IAM, OROS, HELIX, RACER, HELIXCAN, Helixwap.

**(b) AI Infrastructure Validation Layer** - software-first validation of power, compute,
storage, cooling, networks, capacity and AI-readiness. Feeds HelixAtlas, OROS, HelixShield.

**(c) Cross-Ledger Execution Intelligence & Cryptographic Evolution** - consensus evolution,
cryptographic evolution (PQC migration, ML-KEM, Falcon, SHA evolution, signature
aggregation, threshold cryptography, zero-knowledge), interoperability, governance models,
modular infrastructure.

Deliverable per technology studied: an **engineering receipt** carrying architecture
summary, strengths, weaknesses, dependency graph, migration complexity, adoption readiness,
long-term risks, ecosystem impact, cross-industry applications and a recommended monitoring
interval.

Zircon extracts reusable engineering lessons; it does not promote chains. JANUS classifies
without ideology; vLOID stays orthogonal - admissibility happens before anything reaches
consensus.

## Commercial framing observed 2026-08-07 (extends program c)
CQTM, an actively managed quantum ETF launched May 2026, ~30 holdings across quantum
hardware, cryogenics, software and quantum-resilient security, roughly $12-16M AUM.

The phrase that matters: *companies building the quantum future and defending against it*.
Two markets, and only one is ours.

**Not ours:** quantum hardware, cooling, control electronics, compilers, cloud access. We
cannot compete there and should not pretend otherwise.

**Possibly ours:** cryptographic transition evidence. Organisations will not merely need
new cryptography. They will need to know where the old cryptography remains, which systems
depend on it, what can safely be upgraded, what broke during migration, who approved the
change, and whether the completed transition can be independently proven.

## The transferable question
An ETF is an investment wrapper around a sector. The opportunity lies beneath the wrapper.
Zircon should ask of any emerging category:

    what operational problem will every company in this category eventually have,
    that none of the investment marketing describes?

For quantum, the answer may be cryptographic transition evidence. Asked of agentic
payments, prediction markets or AI agents - categories closer to current work - the same
question would produce something actionable sooner.

## Grading
Zircon as an organisation is far downstream. The dependency-intelligence method and the
transferable question above cost nothing and can be applied to current work today.
